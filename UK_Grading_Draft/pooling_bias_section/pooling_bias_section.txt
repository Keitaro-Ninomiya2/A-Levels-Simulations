Why pooling across school types biases logit estimates

In several applications it is tempting to pool all schools and impose common
slope parameters (including the pandemic-year interaction slopes) across heterogeneous school types. This restriction is innocuous in linear models under
standard exogeneity assumptions, but in nonlinear likelihood models it generally produces misspecification and hence inconsistent slope estimates when
the conditional response function differs systematically by type.
